About

My work centers on human mobility and the urban systems it shapes. At NYU, I work with Prof. Takahiro Yabe in the Resilient Urban Networks Lab and with Prof. Mingzhen Lu in the Lu Lab. Before NYU, I was a research assistant in the UCLA Mobility Lab with Prof. Jiaqi Ma.

Across these projects I combine mechanistic mobility models, probabilistic inference, and deep generative methods with large-scale mobility data—with particular attention to what such data can and cannot reveal about the people and places they represent.

Research interests

Research Experience

  1. New York University Graduate research

    Resilient Urban Networks Lab

    Advisor: Prof. Takahiro Yabe

    Disaster-aware modeling of human mobility: understanding how people adapt their movement during hurricanes and other disruptions, and developing spatiotemporal generative models that can capture those adaptations.

    Approach Evaluating mechanistic exploration and preferential return (EPR) models alongside diffusion-based and discrete sequence generative approaches, using large-scale aggregated mobility data.

    • EPR models
    • Diffusion models
    • Discrete sequence models
    • Aggregated mobility data
  2. New York University Graduate research

    Lu Lab

    Advisor: Prof. Mingzhen Lu

    Global human mobility and the population dynamics of rural, urban, and migrant groups—how people distribute themselves across places and how those patterns can be measured at a global scale.

    Approach Inferring recurring locations from observed mobility traces, characterizing how observation coverage shapes what the data reveal, and modeling spatial interaction between places.

    • Recurring-location inference
    • Observation coverage
    • Spatial interaction models
  3. UCLA Research Assistant 2024–2025

    UCLA Mobility Lab

    Advisor: Prof. Jiaqi Ma

    Large-scale human mobility analysis, including probabilistic inference of activities and locations, evaluation of privacy–utility trade-offs, and synthetic mobility generation.

    Approach Built geospatial data pipelines and applied hidden Markov models with Viterbi decoding to infer activity types and points of interest from trajectory data; contributed to work on synthetic population and trajectory generation (PopTraj preprint).

    • Hidden Markov models
    • Activity & POI inference
    • Privacy–utility evaluation
    • Synthetic mobility

Ongoing NYU M.S. capstone

Active Experimental Design for Latent Neural Dynamics

Can targeted perturbations make it more efficient to identify the latent dynamics behind neural population activity? We study this on synthetic low-dimensional dynamical systems with known ground truth.

Details

My part integrates a synthetic latent-dynamics generator with a nonlinear dynamical system (NLDS) estimator, builds controlled baselines that compare passive data, stimulated data with known inputs, and stimulated data without inputs, and runs reproducible multi-seed training and evaluation on NYU’s HPC cluster.

  • System identification
  • Active learning
  • Latent dynamical systems
  • PyTorch

With Eric Huang · 2026

GitHub Issue Resolution Prediction

Predicting how quickly GitHub issues are resolved, using 8.69 million issues from GH Archive under a temporal train–test split.

Details

Compares TF-IDF and repository-signal baselines, fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3, and a hybrid that prepends LLM-generated issue summaries. Fine-tuned text models outperform lexical baselines; repository signals add modest gains, and LLM summaries aid interpretability without improving accuracy.

  • DeBERTa-v3
  • BigQuery
  • Temporal evaluation
